What is an ATS Resume Score & What Does It Mean?

An ATS Resume Score is a numerical rating (typically calculated from 0 to 100) generated by an Applicant Tracking System (such as Workday, Taleo, Greenhouse, or Lever) or an ATS audit scanner. It reflects how effectively automated scanning software can read, categorize, and match your resume against the target job criteria.

When recruiters receive hundreds of applications for an open vacancy, they configure automated filter thresholds. Resumes with a high ATS score (typically 80% or higher) are placed into the top candidate queue for human review. Resumes scoring below this threshold are often filtered out before a human hiring manager ever reads a single bullet point.

The 5 Formatting Traps That Cause Immediate ATS Rejection

1. Multi-Column Tables & Text Boxes

Many graphic templates (especially from Canva) place text into side-by-side floating frames. ATS parsers read horizontally across columns, interleaving dates, company names, and bullet points into an unreadable scrambled mess.

2. Flattened Images & Graphic Skill Bars

Skill rating stars (e.g. "Python: 4/5 stars") or charts are rendered as images. Because ATS parsers cannot read vector graphics, your proficiency in that skill is registered as 0% keyword coverage.

3. Contact Information in Headers/Footers

Many document processors isolate header and footer content from the main text body. If your email or phone number is in the Word or PDF header, the ATS may mark your contact details as missing entirely.

4. Non-Standard Section Titles

ATS engines search for standardized heading anchors like "Work Experience", "Education", and "Skills". Creative titles like "My Professional Path" or "Things I Love Doing" prevent the parser from cataloging your background.
